A **double round robin**: every player meets every other twice, once with each colour. Eight players make fourteen rounds and 56 games. The example is the FIDE Candidates Tournament 2026.

## The format

With everyone playing everyone, there is no pairing to get lucky with, and the score alone says a lot. Ties are broken by Sonneborn-Berger: the points of the opponents a player beat, plus half the points of those they drew with. Buchholz, the sum of all the opponents' points, is left out, because in a round robin everyone's opponents are nearly the same.

## The crosstable

A row and a column per player, in rank order. Where a row meets a column, the cell holds every game between the two players, in round order: two results in a double round robin. The diagonal, a player against themselves, is blank.

Each result is `1`, `½` or `0` from the row player's side. A screen reader reads it in full, for example "Round 4, White against Caruana, Fabiano: win".
