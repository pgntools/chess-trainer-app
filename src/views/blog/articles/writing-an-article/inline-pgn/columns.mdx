`<InlinePgnGame2colH>` is `<InlinePgnGame>` with its moves laid out the way the Analysis Board's move list lays them out: a row per move number — the number, White's move, Black's move — instead of one wrapping run, beside the board. `<InlinePgnGame2colV>` puts the same list under the board. Both take the same props and read the same sources, so any `<InlinePgnGame>` can become either by changing its name. (`<InlinePgnGameColumns>`, `<InlinePgnGame2colH>`'s first name, still works.)

## The list

Each side line is a row of its own under the pair it answers, spanning the width; a side line's own side lines stay inside its run. A window that opens on Black's move starts with an empty White cell.

## A long game

The list is never taller than the board. A window with more moves than fit scrolls inside its own box — the board and the buttons stay put — and stepping to a move brings it into view; a short window takes only the height it needs.

<InlinePgnGame2colH pgn={game} caption="The whole game, its moves scrolling beside the board" />

```mdx
<InlinePgnGame2colH pgn={game} caption="…" />
```

Under a narrow window the board and its list stack, and the list is capped at the board's width — still a square's height. The moves stay left to right in every language, as the board does.

## The moves under the board

`<InlinePgnGame2colV>` stacks the list under the board at every width, as wide as the board and no taller than half of it, scrolling the same way. It is the one for boards side by side: two positions of a game, compared in a `<BoardRow>`.
