A lichess study draws on its board — arrows for moves and plans, circles round squares — and keeps the drawing in the PGN, inside the move's comment. `<InlinePgnGame>` draws them back: whatever a position's comment draws is on the board when the board is at that position.

The boards below are the chapters of the lichess study *General Guidelines on Drawing Arrows* by CreativeBro, its PGN exported as it is. Each chapter is one game of the file, picked with `game`.

## How a PGN writes them

Two commands, inside a comment:

```
{ [%cal Ge2e4,Rd7d5] }   arrows: a brush, then the from and to squares
{ [%csl Gd4,Re5] }       circles: a brush, then the square
```

The brush is one letter — `G` green, `R` red, `Y` yellow (lichess's orange), `B` blue. The comment after a move draws on the position that move reaches; a comment before the first move draws on the starting position. On lichess, a right-click drag draws in green, with Shift in red, with Alt in blue, and with both in orange.

Where a position carries a drawing, the drawing is the board's message: the arrows to the next moves step aside, and the moves stay in the list beside the board.

## The props

- `game` — which game of a PGN holding several, counted from 1. A lichess study exports one game per chapter.
- `shapes={false}` — leave the drawings off, and show the arrows to the next moves instead.
- `comments` — the comment's words under the moves; the drawing commands are taken out of them.

The colours are the theme's: each theme has its four brushes.
