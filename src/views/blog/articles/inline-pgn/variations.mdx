A game under review has its side lines — what could have been played, and what would have followed. `<InlinePgnGame>` shows the side lines that branch inside its window, each nested under the move it replaces, and the board's arrows show them too: green the game's move, blue a side line's.

## Side lines in the window

Capablanca's notes to moves 14 to 17 hold two side lines: 14... gxf6, which he considered, and 16. Bg2, which he expected — with his own combination against it, and its two branches.

<InlinePgnGame pgn={game} from="13..." to="17" start="15..." />

```mdx
<InlinePgnGame pgn={game} from="13..." to="17" start="15..." />
```

## Opening inside a side line

`start` can be a line of moves into a side line. A line that begins at a later move — `16. Bg2 Ne5` — starts from the game's own position before that move, so only the side line needs writing:

<InlinePgnGame pgn={game} from="15..." to="17" start="16. Bg2 Ne5" caption="The combination Capablanca had in mind: 16. Bg2 Ne5!" />

```mdx
<InlinePgnGame pgn={game} from="15..." to="17" start="16. Bg2 Ne5" caption="…" />
```

## The mainline alone

`variations={false}` leaves the side lines out — of the list and of the board.

<InlinePgnGame pgn={game} from="13..." to="17" start="15..." variations={false} />

```mdx
<InlinePgnGame pgn={game} from="13..." to="17" start="15..." variations={false} />
```

## With the game's comments

`comments` shows the PGN comment of the move on the board, under the moves — here Capablanca's own, on 16. Kg2.
