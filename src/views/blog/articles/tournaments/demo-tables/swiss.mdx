A **Swiss**: every round, players are paired against others on the same score, or as near to it as the pairing allows. Nobody is knocked out, and nobody meets the same opponent twice. A field of over a hundred can be ranked in nine rounds, far fewer than a round robin would need. The example is the 112th British Championship, played in Warwick from 1 to 9 August 2026.

## The format

Because players on the same score met different opponents, a tie on points is broken by how strong those opponents were. **Buchholz** is the sum of the opponents' points. **Sonneborn-Berger** is the points of the opponents a player beat, plus half the points of those they drew with.

The file holds 468 games over nine rounds, a few short of every board, so a player with no game in the file in some round has a dash there. A dash is not a bye: the table shows what the file has. Shreyas Royal won the title with 7½ points from 9, ahead of Luke McShane and Michael Adams on 7.

## The standings

A row per player, ranked by points, then Buchholz, then Sonneborn-Berger, with a cell per round. A cell shows the result only; its round, colour and opponent are in its name, for example "Round 3, White against …: win".
